The vowel triangle

Front vowels unrounded (left edge), back vowels rounded (right edge) — that correlation is the two long sides. The five cardinals sit on the edges; a rounded/unrounded pair shares each spot; schwa is the dead centre. Nasalisation rides on top as a separate layer.

17 oral points + 4 nasal (French) · bases i e a o u · operators c j q x · base y retired

Reading it

  • Each spot holds a base·c pair. Front i·y, e·ø · back ɯ·u, ɤ·o · and the apex a·ɑ — so ø sits by e and ɤ by o, not in the middle.
  • c = toggle rounding — front gains it (ic=y, ec=ø), back loses it (uc=ɯ, oc=ɤ); at the apex, where rounding is moot, it marks the back one (ac=ɑ).
  • j = lax — ij=ɪ, ej=ɛ, uj=ʊ, oj=ɔ · q = c+j (front-round lax): iq=ʏ, eq=œ.
  • x = centralise → ə (ax): the featureless centre — which is why it needs no base letter.
  • Why ʏ and œ stay: French contrasts ø/œ (jeûne≠jeune) with no vowel length to recover a merge, and German needs y/ʏ, ø/œ — so q earns its keep. Only ɒ→ɔ merges cleanly.

The nasal layer · a plane on top, not new points

Nasalisation is the h modifier riding on any vowel (ah=ã) — orthogonal to the triangle. In the 16 languages only French carries it, on the four vowels flagged ˜ above.

Changed from the trapezoid: ɒ→ɔ merged (HU ⟨a⟩ → oj) — the only lossless merge · y dropped as a base (schwa is ax) · ɯ, ɤ re-derived from their rounded twins (ix ex → uc oc) · ɑ → ac, freeing x from meaning "back". Reverted: œ→ø and ʏ→y — the injectivity check caught them collapsing real French (jeûne/jeune) and German contrasts, so q stays.
