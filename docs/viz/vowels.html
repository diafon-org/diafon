What a "point" is

A point is the bare skeleton of a sound — nothing extra. For a vowel it's just three facts: how high the tongue sits, how far forward it is, and whether the lips round. Two vowels from different languages are the same point when those facts match.

Anything else — holding it longer, sending air through the nose, adding an "r" colour — is a modifier that rides on top. Modifiers never make a new point; they decorate one. That's the whole trick: a handful of points, plus a few modifiers, instead of hundreds of one-off symbols.

A vowel point = height · backness · round

Height = how close the tongue is to the roof (i high, a low). Backness = front (i) vs back (u). Round = are the lips rounded (u) or spread (i). The vowel chart mirrors a side view of the mouth.
