The operators

Five letters are never bases — they are the operators, and each does one job on a consonant and another on a vowel. That is the whole system; "long / aspirated / palatalised" are just names for what these do.

opon a consonanton a vowel
caffricate — tc=t͡sround — ic=y
qhush — tq=t͡ʃ, sq=ʃround+lower — iq=ʏ
xdental/velar — tx=θ, kx=xback — ix=ɯ
jpalatal — tj=c, nj=ɲlower — ij=ɪ
wlong — tw=tːlong — iw=iː

Aspiration is a sixth, part-time operator: h (ph=pʰ). Two stacked operators appear only for genuinely two-feature sounds — tch=t͡sʰ, tqx=tˤ.

Vowels · 18 points · bases a e i o u y(=ə)

c round and j lower are primitive; q is their combination c+j = round+lower — exactly as on the consonants. Every vowel is base or base+1op.

y=ə is the central vowel — and it also carries English STRUT ʌ and Korean ㅓ, so it is not idle.

Reading it

t͡ʃ→tq
Italian cielo — hush affricate (merged with alveolo-palatal t͡ɕ).
ʂ→sq
Mandarin shān — retroflex folds into the hush, no sign (sh/x are complementary).
c · tʲ→tj
Hungarian ty = Russian soft ть — palatal, one letter.
ʏ→iq
German lax ü (Glück) — round+lower (q = c+j).
t͈→tw
Korean fortis — reuses length; no vowel-length contrast to clash with.
ə→y
English schwa — the 6th base, so the = dxy.
